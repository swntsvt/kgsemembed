\section{Which annotations help: a predicate-role ablation}
\label{app:roles}

Section~\ref{sec:ppas} holds the representation fixed. Here we vary its
content. Every predicate of D1 and D2 is assigned one role, fixed from
property declarations and sample values before any run: \emph{core}
(\texttt{rdfs:label}, \texttt{rdf:type}, \texttt{rdfs:subClassOf}),
preferred name, synonym, definition, identifier, non-English, disjointness,
or other. With M2 and the key--value representation, arms remove one role
from both sides (R-$x$), keep only core (A-core), or add one role to core
(C+$x$); A0 keeps everything. Without a budget, only the model's 512-token
limit applies (no input reached it).

Core text alone ranks best on both datasets (Table~\ref{tab:roles}), and
the single-role removals do not add up to that gain, so it does not come
from one role. Adding synonyms or definitions to core text hurts on both
datasets, which is consistent with the drop in V1/V2 MRR once those
predicates were read correctly. Under a budget the roles interact with the
token limit: on D2, removing synonyms gains more under a budget than without
one (difference-in-differences $+0.063$ [$0.043$, $0.081$] at 24 tokens),
while removing definitions loses its gain ($-0.076$ [$-0.090$, $-0.062$]).
Selected items are written in a fixed order, and part of these effects may
depend on where an item appears in the text rather than on its content; we
leave that question to separate work.
